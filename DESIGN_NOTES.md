# Design Notes

The game focuses on readable musical causality: a performer should visually suggest how its sound happens. Percussion insects visibly strike or pulse body parts; wing performers move wings; chime performers move forelegs or tail structures; reed performers emphasize the proboscis; chord and drone performers use broader sustained movement.

The progression curve changes musical density rather than simply increasing prices. Moss Meadow is rhythm-first and sparse. Rainroot adds flowing plucks and reeds. Ember adds bass and heavier shell transients. Frostwing uses long spacing and bell resonance. Aurora Hive introduces arpeggios and major-color harmony. Tempest Crown uses faster tempo, dense syncopation, thunder percussion, charged leads and sustained chord support.

Final castle capacity is deliberately far above the total unique species-bed cost for each island so the player can fit all native species and still keep duplicates or variants.
